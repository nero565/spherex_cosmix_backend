Search any place in the sky, fly around it, then blink real SPHEREx observations to see what moves or changes brightness.

Drag to rotate · scroll to zoom · click a target to fly there · double-click to open its telescope view

Pick a target

Click one of the glowing dots (or a button above). The camera flies there and shows its distance from Earth, its motion on the sky, and a button that opens the telescope images.

Distances use a logarithmic scale so near and far targets fit on one screen. Rings mark 10, 100 and 1000 light-years.

Same place, different decades: each button loads a real all-sky survey taken in that era. Dates are approximate survey periods, and coverage varies by survey.

Drag to move, scroll to zoom (the whole sky is explorable), click to set the target. Then use “Look here in SPHEREx”.

Search for an object or pick an example, and the SPHEREx images will appear here.

This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), which is a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, and is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration. Data served by NASA/IPAC IRSA. Distances and proper motions of the example targets: rounded literature values (Gaia-based where available); the two variable stars have approximate distances. Name lookup: CDS Sesame. What you see on a frame are candidates for motion or change, not confirmed discoveries; noise and image quality can look like movement. Sky map: Aladin Lite (CDS).
